# Parenting Goals & Family Milestones

**Purpose**: Your family's specific goals and what you're working toward

**Remember**: These aren't rigid checklists. They're North Stars to guide your approach.

---

## Core Family Goals

### 1. Sleep Habits

**Vision**: Long sleep periods established early and maintained

**For [Child] (toddler, 2-3 years old)**:
- **Current Success**: Sleeps at a consistent morning wake time most days
- **Maintain**: Consistent bedtime routine, handle regressions with 12 Hours toolbox
- **Continue**: Protecting sleep as non-negotiable

**For Second Child**:
- **Goal**: 12 hours of sleep by 12 weeks old
- **Method**: Same approach that worked with your first child
- **Commit**: Use nighttime & daytime toolboxes consistently
- **Track**: Log feeds and sleep in early weeks

**Why It Matters** (from NurtureShock):
- Sleep is foundational for development
- One hour less = 2 years lost cognitive development
- Works WITH your routines (not against them)

---

### 2. Autonomy & Independence

**Vision**: Kids as team members on the field, not spectators in the stands

**Emotional Independence**:
- Self-regulate feelings
- Recover from upsets
- Ask for help when needed (not helpless)

**Physical Independence**:
- Do age-appropriate tasks themselves
- Contribute to family (comedido)
- Try before asking for help

**For Your Toddler (Current Focus)**:
- Dress themselves (mostly)
- Help with real family tasks (setting table, cleaning up)
- Notice what needs to be done without being told
- Express feelings with words

**For Both Kids Long-Term**:
- See themselves as capable
- Want to be independent (not forced)
- Separation of self from parents

---

### 3. Mirroring Your Values

**Vision**: Kids understand and reflect what they see you model

**Values to Model**:
- **Passion**: Pursuing interests with enthusiasm
- **Hard Work**: Persistence through challenges
- **Quality**: Caring about how things are done
- **Respect**: For people, relationships, commitments
- **Relationships**: Healthy partnership, friendships, community

**How They Show It**:
- Talk about their own interests excitedly
- Don't give up when things are hard
- Care about doing things well
- Treat others kindly
- Value family and friendships

**The Best Feedback**:
When they reflect your values back to you through a compliment or something - that's the best thing on the planet.

---

### 4. Working WITH Family Routines

**Vision**: Kids adapt to healthy family habits, not chaos revolving around them

**Routines**:
- **Sleep**: Kids sleep during a consistent 12-hour block
- **Meals**: Family eats together at set times
- **Activities**: Family-centered, not solely child-centered
- **Adult Time**: Protected couple time, parent friendships

**Why It Matters** (from Bringing Up Bebe & Danish Way):
- Preserves parent identity and energy
- Kids benefit from seeing functional adult life
- Sustainable for long-term
- Kids thrive with structure

---

### 5. Secure Attachment

**Vision**: Kids trust you as their safe base

**What It Looks Like**:
- Comfortable exploring (because they know you're there)
- Come to you when hurt or scared
- Confident in your love (not anxious or people-pleasing)
- Can handle separation age-appropriately

**How You Build It**:
- Respond to needs consistently (especially in infancy)
- Be emotionally available
- Repair when you mess up
- Co-regulate their big feelings

---

### 6. Strong Self-Worth (Not Self-Esteem)

**Vision**: Kids know their worth through contribution, not external validation

**Difference** (from Hunt, Gather, Parent):
- **Self-esteem**: Cultural creation, external validation-dependent
- **Self-worth**: Intrinsic sense of value through competence and contribution

**How You Build It**:
- Let them contribute to family (real tasks)
- Acknowledge helpfulness (not praise)
- Let them struggle and succeed
- Competence > constant affirmation

**Not**:
- Constant praise and "good job!"
- Inflating ego
- Shielding from all failure

## Current Developmental Focus

### Toddler (2-3 Years Old)

**Working On**:
1. **Comedido** (noticing what needs to be done)
   - Including them in real work
   - Activating help through hints
   - "Let's clean up together"

2. **Emotional Regulation**
   - Naming feelings
   - Calming from upsets faster
   - Using words instead of tantrums

3. **Language & Communication**
   - Expanding vocabulary
   - Asking questions, getting thoughtful answers
   - Expressing needs clearly

4. **Self-Care Skills**
   - Dressing (mostly independent)
   - Using utensils well
   - Washing hands
   - Brushing teeth (with help)

5. **Social Skills**
   - Empathy for others
   - Sharing and turn-taking
   - Gentle touch

---

### New Baby (Not Yet Born)

**First 3 Months**:
1. **Sleep Foundation**
   - Day/night distinction
   - The Pause technique
   - Self-soothing skills

2. **Feeding Rhythm**
   - Moving toward 4 feeds/day
   - Eliminating night feeds by 8-12 weeks

3. **Secure Attachment**
   - Responsive care
   - Consistent routine
   - Building trust

**Months 3-6**:
1. **12 Hours Sleep Maintained**
   - Naps solidifying
   - Sleep regressions handled with toolbox

2. **Physical Development**
   - Tummy time
   - Reaching, grasping
   - Rolling

3. **Social Smiles & Engagement**
   - Recognizing faces
   - Responding to voices
   - Early babbling

## What Success ISN'T

**Not About**:
- Hitting standardized milestones on schedule
- Being "the best" or comparing to other kids
- Perfect behavior
- Never making mistakes
- Kids who never struggle or fail

**Remember** (from Hunt, Gather, Parent):
> "Personalized needs instead of standardized development milestones."

Every child's timeline is their own.

---

## Milestones You're NOT Chasing

**Things You Don't Care About** (cultural pressure, but not your values):

- Early academic achievement (reading at 3, etc.)
- Being "gifted" or special
- Scheduled activities and achievements
- Perfect manners and compliance
- Never crying or having big feelings

**Why**: These are cultural constructs, not indicators of healthy development or family success.

## Progress Check: How You'll Know It's Working

### Sleep

- Kids sleep through the night consistently
- Wake up rested, in good moods
- Bedtime isn't a battle
- Regressions are temporary and manageable

### Autonomy

- Kids try things before asking for help
- Show pride in doing things themselves
- Contribute to family without being forced
- Comfortable exploring (age-appropriately)

### Values Mirroring

- They talk about things they're passionate about
- They persist through challenges
- They treat others with respect
- You see YOUR behaviors reflected back

### Emotional Regulation

- Big feelings still happen (that's normal)
- They recover from upsets faster over time
- They can name feelings
- They come to you when they need help

### Family Routines

- Routines feel natural, not forced
- Kids adapt to family rhythm
- You maintain adult time and identity
- Life feels sustainable, not chaotic

## Long-Term Vision (5-10 Years Out)

**What kind of kids are you raising?**

- **Capable**: Can do things for themselves
- **Helpful**: Notice what needs to be done and do it
- **Resilient**: Bounce back from setbacks
- **Empathetic**: Care about others' feelings
- **Confident**: Trust their abilities through experience
- **Connected**: Secure attachment to parents and healthy relationships
- **Independent**: Emotionally and physically
- **Value-Driven**: Understand passion, hard work, quality, respect

**What kind of family are you building?**

- **Team-Oriented**: Everyone contributes
- **Connected**: Hygge, togetherness, secure bonds
- **Sustainable**: Parents maintain identity and energy
- **Community-Oriented**: Alloparent network, not isolated
- **Calm**: Emotional regulation modeled and taught

---

## Adjusting Goals Over Time

**These goals will evolve**:
- As kids grow, priorities shift
- What works at 2 may not work at 7
- Some goals get easier, new challenges emerge

**How to Adjust**:
1. **Reflect regularly**: What's working? What's not?
2. **Stay aligned with values**: Even if tactics change
3. **Be flexible**: Rigid goals create stress
4. **Communicate with partner**: Check in, adjust together

**Remember**: Goals guide you. They don't define you.

---

## When You Need a Reminder

**On hard days, remember WHY**:

- You're building autonomy (not controlling them)
- You're teaching regulation (not perfect behavior)
- You're modeling values (not lecturing about them)
- You're creating team members (not obedient servants)
- You're preserving adult life (so you have energy for the long haul)

**You're playing the long game. This moment is part of the process.**
