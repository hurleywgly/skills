# Birth Partner - Medical Reference Guide

**Purpose**: Evidence-based medical reference for pregnancy, labor, delivery, and postpartum
**Context**: "Short shelf life" - Most relevant during pregnancy and first 3 months postpartum
**Based on**: ACOG 2024 clinical guidelines, WHO recommendations, peer-reviewed evidence

**Note**: This complements (not replaces) "The Birth Partner, 5th Edition" which you own. Use both together.

---

## Labor Stages & What to Expect

### First Stage: Early Labor to Full Dilation

**Definition** (ACOG 2024):
- Begins: Onset of regular, painful contractions causing cervical change
- Ends: Full dilation (10 cm)

**Phases**:
- **Latent/Early Phase**: 0-6 cm (can be long, irregular)
- **Active Phase**: 6-10 cm (ACOG defines active at 6cm, not 4cm as previously thought)

**What's Normal**:
- Early phase can last hours to days (especially first baby)
- Contractions become longer, stronger, closer together
- Cervix dilates and effaces (thins)

**When to Go to Hospital/Birth Center**:
- Contractions 5 minutes apart, lasting 1 minute, for 1 hour (5-1-1 rule)
- Water breaks (especially if fluid isn't clear)
- Bleeding more than light spotting
- Decreased fetal movement
- Trust your instinct

---

### Second Stage: Pushing to Birth

**Definition** (ACOG 2024):
- Begins: Full dilation (10 cm)
- Ends: Baby is born

**What's Normal**:
- **First baby**: Up to 3 hours of pushing
- **Subsequent babies**: Up to 2 hours of pushing
- Can be longer with epidural

**Partner's Role**:
- Support position changes
- Encouragement
- Breathing/pushing coaching (follow medical team's lead)
- Advocate for birthing person's preferences

---

### Third Stage: Delivery of Placenta

**Timeline**: Usually 5-30 minutes after baby is born

**What Happens**:
- Uterus continues contracting
- Placenta detaches
- Healthcare provider may gently pull cord while mother pushes

**Medical Team Watches For**: Excessive bleeding (postpartum hemorrhage)

---

### Fourth Stage: Immediate Postpartum (First 2 Hours)

**Why It Matters**: Highest risk time for maternal complications

**What's Monitored**:
- Bleeding/uterine tone
- Blood pressure
- Vital signs
- Bonding and feeding initiation

## Pain Management Options

### Non-Pharmacological (No Medications)

**Evidence-Based Effective Methods**:

1. **Water Immersion** (Shower/tub)
   - Lowers pain scores
   - No evidence of harm
   - Most effective in active labor

2. **Continuous Support Person**
   - ONE consistent support person (partner, doula, friend)
   - Proven to improve outcomes
   - Reduces cesarean rates, shortens labor

3. **Movement & Position Changes**
   - Walking, squatting, hands/knees, side-lying
   - Helps baby descend
   - Provides distraction

4. **Other Techniques** (varying evidence):
   - Breathing techniques
   - Massage
   - Acupuncture/acupressure
   - Intradermal sterile water injections (for back labor)
   - Relaxation/visualization

---

### Pharmacological (Medications)

#### Neuraxial Anesthesia (Epidural/Spinal)

**ACOG Recommendation**: Should be offered at any stage of labor

**Key Points**:
- Maternal request is sufficient medical indication (you don't need to be a certain dilation)
- Very effective pain relief
- Allows rest during long labor
- Can slow labor slightly (manageable)
- Increases likelihood of assisted delivery (forceps/vacuum) slightly

**Myths Debunked**:
- Does NOT increase cesarean rate (when used appropriately)
- Does NOT cause long-term back pain
- CAN be given early in labor (old "wait until 4cm" rule is outdated)

#### IV Pain Medication

**Options**: Opioids (fentanyl, morphine, others)

**Pros**:
- Takes edge off pain
- Doesn't eliminate sensation (can still move)

**Cons**:
- Can cause nausea, drowsiness
- Crosses placenta (baby may be sleepy at birth, usually temporary)
- Less effective than epidural

## Medical Interventions: When & Why

### Continuous Fetal Monitoring

**Options**:
- **Intermittent auscultation**: Check heart rate periodically with Doppler
- **Continuous electronic**: Belts on abdomen tracking baby's heart rate constantly

**ACOG**: Continuous monitoring for high-risk situations; intermittent acceptable for low-risk

### Amniotomy (Breaking Water)

**When Used**:
- To augment or induce labor
- Shortens labor duration

**Considerations**: Increases risk of infection if labor is prolonged after

### Augmentation (Speeding Labor)

**Methods**:
- **Pitocin** (synthetic oxytocin) via IV
- Amniotomy (breaking water)

**When Used**: Labor has stalled or is progressing very slowly

**What to Know**: Contractions may become stronger/more frequent

### Cesarean Section (C-Section)

**Common Reasons**:
- Labor arrest (labor stops progressing despite interventions)
- Fetal distress (concerning heart rate patterns)
- Breech or other non-optimal positions
- Placenta problems
- Previous cesarean (though VBAC is often possible)

**Recovery**: Major abdominal surgery, 6-8 week recovery

### Operative Vaginal Delivery (Forceps/Vacuum)

**When Used**: Second stage arrest, need to expedite delivery

**ACOG**: Should be assessed before proceeding to cesarean for second-stage arrest

## Complications: Warning Signs

### During Labor

**Call Nurse/Provider Immediately If**:
- Sudden severe pain (different from contractions)
- Heavy bleeding
- Fever >100.4 degrees F
- Severe headache, vision changes
- Decreased fetal movement

### After Delivery (Postpartum)

**Call Healthcare Provider Immediately If**:

#### Bleeding
- Soaking >1 pad/hour
- Clots larger than a quarter
- Blood flow that increases after slowing

#### Fever & Infection
- Fever >=100.4 degrees F
- Foul-smelling discharge
- Increasing pain, redness, swelling at incision (C-section/episiotomy)

#### Cardiovascular
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Fast or irregular heartbeat
- Swelling/redness/warmth in one leg (blood clot signs)

#### Neurological
- Severe headache that won't go away
- Vision changes, seeing spots
- Seizure

#### Abdominal
- Severe or persistent abdominal pain

#### Breast (Mastitis)
- Red, hot, painful breast
- Flu-like symptoms with breast pain
- Red streaks on breast

#### Urinary
- Pain/burning when urinating
- Unable to urinate

#### Mental Health
- Thoughts of harming self or baby
- Severe sadness/hopelessness >10 days
- Inability to care for self or baby

**Trust Your Gut**: If something feels wrong, call. Better to check than wait.

## Postpartum Recovery (Fourth Trimester)

### Physical Recovery Timeline

**Week 1-2**:
- Vaginal bleeding (lochia) - heavy, like period
- Cramping (afterpains) especially while breastfeeding
- Soreness (perineal if vaginal birth, incision if C-section)
- Breast engorgement (days 2-5 as milk comes in)
- Exhaustion

**Week 3-6**:
- Bleeding lightens (pink to brown to yellow)
- Energy slowly returns
- Soreness decreases
- 6-week postpartum checkup

**Month 2-3**:
- Most physical healing complete
- Still adjusting to sleep deprivation
- Hormones still stabilizing

### What's Normal Postpartum

- Night sweats (hormones)
- Hair loss (around 3 months)
- Emotional ups and downs (first 2 weeks especially)
- Breast milk leaking
- Vaginal dryness (especially if breastfeeding)
- Hemorrhoids, constipation

### C-Section Specific

- Incision healing: 6-8 weeks
- No lifting >baby's weight for 6 weeks
- No driving while on pain meds
- Scar massage (once healed) helps long-term
- Higher infection risk - watch incision closely

## Breastfeeding Medical Basics

### Normal

- Frequent feeding (8-12+ times/24 hours in early weeks)
- Cluster feeding (many feeds close together)
- Some soreness initially (should improve within 1 week)
- Milk comes in day 2-5 (breasts get full, hard, warm)

### Complications Requiring Support

**Mastitis** (breast infection):
- Red, hot, painful area on breast
- Fever, flu-like symptoms
- Treatment: Antibiotics, continued nursing, rest
- Do NOT stop nursing - it makes it worse

**Thrush** (yeast infection):
- Shiny, itchy, burning nipples
- White patches in baby's mouth
- Treatment: Antifungal for mom and baby

**Poor Latch**:
- Extreme nipple pain beyond first week
- Cracked, bleeding nipples
- Baby not gaining weight
- Seek lactation consultant ASAP

**Low Supply** (true low supply is rare):
- Baby not gaining weight
- <6 wet diapers/day after day 5
- Consult lactation consultant before supplementing

## Partner's Advocacy Role

### Questions to Ask Healthcare Providers

**During Labor**:
- "What are our options right now?"
- "What are the risks and benefits of each?"
- "What happens if we wait?"
- "Is this medically necessary or recommended?"

**For Interventions**:
- "Why is this being recommended now?"
- "Are there alternatives?"
- "Can we have a few minutes to discuss?"

### When to Speak Up

- Preferences in birth plan aren't being followed (unless medical necessity)
- Birthing person's questions/concerns are dismissed
- You feel information isn't being clearly communicated
- Birthing person is too exhausted to self-advocate

### How to Advocate

- Stay calm and respectful
- Ask questions, request explanations
- Request second opinions when appropriate
- Remember: Medical team wants healthy outcomes too

## When to Seek Help: Quick Reference

| Symptom | Urgency |
|---|---|
| Decreased fetal movement (pregnancy) | Call provider now |
| Water breaks | Call provider (usually come in within hours) |
| Contractions 5-1-1 | Go to hospital/birth center |
| Heavy bleeding (soaking >1 pad/hour postpartum) | Call provider immediately |
| Fever >=100.4 degrees F (postpartum) | Call provider same day |
| Chest pain, difficulty breathing | Call 911 |
| Severe headache, vision changes | Call provider immediately |
| Thoughts of harming self/baby | Call provider/mental health crisis line immediately |
| Breast red, hot, painful + fever | Call provider same day (likely mastitis) |

## Key Principles

1. **Labor is variable** - Every birth is different, timelines vary
2. **You have options** - Ask questions, understand choices
3. **Medical team is your ally** - Work together for best outcome
4. **Trust your instincts** - If something feels wrong, speak up
5. **Recovery takes time** - Be patient with yourself
6. **Ask for help** - Medical, practical, emotional - don't tough it out alone
