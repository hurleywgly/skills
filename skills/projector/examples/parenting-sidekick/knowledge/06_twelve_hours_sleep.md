# Twelve Hours' Sleep by Twelve Weeks Old

**Book**: Twelve Hours' Sleep by Twelve Weeks Old by Suzy Giordano & Lisa Abidin
**Purpose**: Your North Star for sleep training. Practical toolboxes and tactics to establish healthy sleep habits early and maintain them through regressions.

---

## Core Philosophy

**The Goal**: Baby sleeps 12 hours straight at night by 12 weeks old

**Why It Matters for Your Family**:
- Long sleep habits established early
- Works WITH your routines (not against them)
- Sustainable for parents
- Science-backed (NurtureShock confirms: sleep is foundational)

**Your Outcome with Your First Child**: They sleep at a consistent morning wake time most days. That's awesome. Do it again.

---

## The "Limited-Crying Solution"

**This is NOT "cry it out"** - it's structured, responsive sleep training

**Key Principle**: Baby shouldn't cry more than 5 minutes without you consoling them

**How It Works**:
1. Pause 3-5 minutes before responding to nighttime crying
2. If crying continues, use soothing techniques WITHOUT picking baby up
3. Softly stroke stomach, murmur calming sounds
4. Teach self-soothing while providing reassurance

**Difference from CIO**: You respond, comfort, reassure - but you don't rescue them from learning to self-soothe

---

## The 12-Hour Framework

### Day/Night Distinction

**Teach from Week 1**: Daytime = awake and active. Nighttime = no interaction.

**Daytime**:
- Lights on, normal noise level
- Social interaction during feeds
- Playtime, activity, stimulation

**Nighttime** (12-hour block: 7pm-7am, 8pm-8am, your choice):
- Dark, quiet
- Minimal interaction during feeds (if any)
- Calm, boring
- No play, no talking, just business

**Why It Works**: Circadian rhythm learns when to be alert vs when to sleep

---

## The Feeding Schedule

### Goal: 4 Feeds Per Day (Within 12-Hour Daytime Window)

**Spacing**: 4 hours apart

**No Night Feeds**: Once baby can go 12 hours (usually by 8-12 weeks)

**Example Schedule** (7am-7pm):
- 7:00am - Feed 1
- 11:00am - Feed 2
- 3:00pm - Feed 3
- 7:00pm - Feed 4 (bedtime feed)
- 7:00pm-7:00am - SLEEP (no feeds)

**Track It**: For the first 8 weeks, keep a log of when baby feeds and sleeps. Patterns emerge.

---

## The Bedtime Routine

**Start 30 minutes before final feed**

### Step-by-Step Routine:

1. **Go to nursery** (same place every night)
2. **Lower the lights** (dim, calm)
3. **Nice music** (soft, soothing)
4. **Bath** (warm, relaxing)
5. **Diaper change**
6. **Pajamas**
7. **Read or sing** (brief, calm)
8. **Final feed** (in nursery, lights low)
9. **Put baby in crib AWAKE**

**Critical Step**: Baby goes into crib awake (drowsy but not asleep)

**Why**: Learning to FALL asleep in crib (not just wake up there) = self-soothing skill

---

## The Nighttime Toolbox

**When baby cries at night, try these in order** (before picking up):

### Tool 1: **The Pause**
- Wait 3-5 minutes
- Listen: Is baby escalating or settling?
- Sometimes they self-soothe within 5 minutes

### Tool 2: **Gentle Touch**
- Softly stroke stomach or back
- Murmur calming sounds ("Shh, shh, it's okay")
- DON'T pick up yet

### Tool 3: **Pacifier** (if you use one)
- Offer pacifier to soothe
- Keep environment dark, quiet, boring

### Tool 4: **Check Diaper**
- Quick, efficient change if needed
- No talking, no eye contact, no play
- Right back to crib

### Tool 5: **Feed** (only if truly necessary in early weeks)
- As baby gets older (8+ weeks), eliminate night feeds
- If feeding, keep it businesslike - dark, quiet, efficient

**Progression**: As weeks go on, rely more on Tools 1-3, less on 4-5

## The Daytime Toolbox (Naps)

**When to Start**: About 2 weeks AFTER nighttime sleep is established

**Goal Nap Schedule**:
- Morning nap: 1 hour
- Afternoon nap: 2 hours
- Total: ~3 hours daytime sleep

**Same Principles Apply**:
- Consistent nap times
- Same soothing routine (abbreviated)
- Put down awake
- Use pause before responding

**Why It Matters**: Good daytime sleep supports nighttime sleep (counterintuitive but true)

## Handling Regressions

**Reality**: Sleep will regress. Teething, illness, travel, developmental leaps.

**Your Quote**: "This book is a constant source of reference with the nighttime toolbox, the daytime toolboxes - how to wean back into it when you have a regression week."

### Regression Toolkit

#### 1. **Go Back to Basics**
- Reinforce bedtime routine (every single step)
- Return to strict schedule
- Re-establish day/night distinction

#### 2. **Don't Create New Habits**
- Tempting to rock to sleep, co-sleep, add night feeds
- These become NEW problems to break
- Stick to the method even when it's hard

#### 3. **Use the Toolboxes**
- Nighttime toolbox: pause, gentle touch, soothe without picking up
- Daytime toolbox: consistent naps, same routine

#### 4. **Be Patient but Consistent**
- Regressions usually last 3-7 days if you stay consistent
- Longer if you introduce new sleep crutches

#### 5. **Check for Real Issues**
- Ear infection, teething pain, illness = valid reasons for disruption
- Address medical issues, then return to method

## Week-by-Week Progression

### Weeks 1-2: **Observation & Rhythm**
- Log all feeds and sleep
- Establish day/night distinction
- Begin bedtime routine

### Weeks 3-4: **Stretching Night Sleep**
- Gradually extend time between night feeds
- Use pause and soothing techniques

### Weeks 5-8: **Eliminating Night Feeds**
- Baby should be able to go longer stretches
- Focus on self-soothing during night wakings

### Weeks 9-12: **12-Hour Goal**
- Baby sleeps 12 hours straight
- 4 feeds during daytime only
- Established routine is second nature

### Weeks 13+: **Maintenance & Naps**
- Nighttime sleep is solid
- Refine daytime naps
- Maintain consistency through regressions

## Key Success Factors

### 1. **Consistency is Everything**
- Same bedtime every night
- Same routine every time
- Same responses to night wakings

### 2. **Both Parents Aligned**
- Agree on the method
- Support each other through hard nights
- Don't undermine with "just this once"

### 3. **Awake But Drowsy**
- THE critical skill: baby learns to fall asleep IN the crib
- Not rocked/fed to sleep and then transferred

### 4. **Environment Matters**
- Dark room (blackout curtains)
- Cool temperature (68-72 degrees F)
- White noise (optional but helpful)
- Safe sleep space (firm mattress, no loose items)

### 5. **Trust the Process**
- Some nights will be hard
- Regressions will happen
- The method works if you stick with it

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Inconsistency
- Doing method 3 nights, giving up, trying again
- Creates confusion, not learning

### Too Much Variation
- Different bedtimes every night
- Different people doing different routines

### Waiting Too Long to Respond
- This isn't CIO - 5 minutes is the limit
- Don't let baby cry in distress

### Creating Sleep Crutches
- Rocking to sleep every time
- Feeding to sleep
- Co-sleeping when not planned
- These become dependencies

### Giving Up During Regressions
- Hardest time to stay consistent
- Most important time to stay consistent

## Preparing for a Second Child

**What You Know Works (from your first child)**:
- Consistent routine
- Early sleep habits
- Firm bedtime

**What to Replicate**:
- Same method from Week 1
- Same commitment to consistency
- Same toolboxes during tough nights

**What to Anticipate**:
- Every baby is different (temperament, feeding needs)
- Core method is the same, timing might adjust slightly
- Having done it once makes the second time easier (you trust the process)

## Quick Reference: Troubleshooting

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Baby won't fall asleep in crib | Ensure drowsy but awake. Use pause + soothing touch. Don't give up. |
| Night wakings return | Check: hunger? diaper? temp? If not, use nighttime toolbox. Don't create new habits. |
| Naps are chaos | Start 2 weeks AFTER nighttime is solid. Same principles. Consistency. |
| Regression after travel | Back to basics. Full routine. Toolboxes. Patience. |
| One parent caves | Get on same page. Support each other. Remember the goal. |
