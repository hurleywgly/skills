# Hunt, Gather, Parent - Practical Tactics & Techniques

**Book**: Hunt, Gather, Parent by Michaeleen Doucleff
**Purpose**: Concrete, actionable techniques for cooperation, emotional regulation, and teaching helpfulness

---

## Core Concept: Comedido

**Comedido** (Spanish, from Maya culture): A child who is aware of what needs to be done and does it without being asked.

**The Goal**: Raise children who:
- Notice what needs to be done
- Contribute without being told
- See themselves as part of the family team

**How It Develops**:
- Children work alongside adults
- They observe real work (not just play)
- Tasks are real contributions, not busywork
- Helpfulness is acknowledged, not praised

## Communication: Stop Talking So Much

> "American parents need to **stop talking so much** and listen to their children."

### The Problem with Commands

**Don't Say**: "Put away your plate." / "Do this." / "Don't do that."

**Why It Fails**:
- Creates power struggle
- Trains obedience, not cooperation
- Increases conflict and resistance
- Drowns out what's important

### Alternative Communication Strategies

#### 1. **Questions (Instead of Commands)**

Turn commands into puzzles for the child to solve.

| Instead of... | Try... |
|---|---|
| "Don't climb on the dog!" | "What are you doing to your friend?" |
| "Clean up your toys!" | "What needs to happen before dinner?" |
| "Stop hitting your sister!" | "How is she feeling right now?" |
| "Get ready for bed!" | "What do we do when our bodies are tired?" |

**Purpose**: Prompts children to think about their actions and consequences, rather than blindly comply.

#### 2. **Consequence Puzzles**

Calmly state the natural consequence, then walk away.

| Situation | Consequence Puzzle |
|---|---|
| Climbing on furniture | "You're going to fall off and hurt yourself." (then walk away) |
| Rough play | "If you climb on her back, you will hurt her." |
| Running in dangerous area | "You could trip and get hurt." |

**Key**: Say it once, stay calm, walk away. No lectures, no repetition.

#### 3. **The Look**

> "Take whatever you have to say to a misbehaving child and channel it into your facial expression. Open your eyes wide, scrunch up your nose, shake your head, then look over to the child."

**When to Use**: Minor misbehavior, boundary testing

**Why It Works**:
- No words = no argument
- Clear communication through expression
- Minimal energy, maximum impact

#### 4. **Just Do It (Action Over Words)**

Instead of asking a child to do a task, **just start doing the task yourself**. The kid will follow.

**Example**:
- Don't say: "Come help me set the table."
- Do: Start setting the table. They'll join.

**Why It Works**: Children want to participate in real work. Invitation through action is more effective than verbal commands.

#### 5. **What Am I, Your Donkey?**

A playful question that highlights when a child is treating you like a servant.

**Use When**: Child is being demanding or expecting you to do things they can do themselves

**Purpose**: Gentle redirect that makes them think about the dynamic

## Stop Using Do's and Don'ts

> "This is tough because these words are entrenched in our dialogue with children, but even cutting them out—do's and don'ts—I guarantee you'll argue less."

### The Challenge

"Do this." "Don't do this." "Please do this." "Don't do that."

- Creates constant power struggle
- Positions parent as controller
- Increases resistance

### The Alternative

Use **questions, consequence puzzles, and actions** instead.

**Practice**:
- Catch yourself before saying "Don't..."
- Reframe as a question or consequence statement
- Let silence and action do the work

## Taming Tantrums

### The Core Principle

> "Tantrums go away if we respond to a child with **calmness**. See tantrums as a chance for the child to practice calming themselves down and for you to model calmness, not the time for you as a parent to prove a point."

### The CALM Framework During Tantrums

**C - Calm yourself first**
- Your emotional state sets the tone
- Breathe, pause, stay neutral
- You cannot de-escalate them if you're escalated

**A - Acknowledge without engaging**
- Don't try to teach or lecture mid-tantrum
- Don't negotiate or give in
- Simple acknowledgment: "You're upset. I see that."

**L - Low energy, low words**
> "Whenever children are upset, screaming, the parents say very few words. They make very few movements, and they show very little expression."

**M - Model calmness, then address**
> "When a child is too upset to find the parent, the child is too emotionally charged to listen. So there's no reason to try to teach the child a big lesson in those moments. You have to remain calm and wait for the child to calm down. **Then** you teach the child."

### The Maria Effect

> "Notice how calm and relaxed the adults are, how profound this impact, this calmness has in the house. Maria's calm reigns. Rosie's fire cools, and anger eases."

**Your calm is contagious. So is your chaos.**

### What NOT to Do

- Lecture or explain during tantrum
- Try to teach lessons when they're escalated
- Match their emotional intensity
- Give in to stop the tantrum

### What TO Do

- Minimal words
- Minimal movement
- Minimal expression
- Wait for them to calm
- Then, if needed, address calmly

## Teaching Tasks & Cooperation

### The Three Rules for Tasks

1. **Tasks should be doable** - Match their skill level, not beyond
2. **Tasks should be real** - Actual contribution to family, not busywork
3. **Never force a task** - Cooperation, not obedience

> "If a child says no or ignores you, leave it alone. Try again later. Training the child to cooperate, not to obey the parent."

### Frame Tasks as Communal Activity

| Instead of... | Try... |
|---|---|
| "Put away your plate after dinner." | "Let's all work together to clean up the kitchen after dinner." |
| "Fold your laundry." | "Let's all help fold the laundry together as a family." |
| "Go clean your room." | "I'm starting to tidy up. Want to help?" |

### Activate Help Through Hints (Not Commands)

> "Instead of explicitly telling the child to do a task, activate their help by telling them you're starting a chore or giving them a hint that the chore is needed."

**Examples**:
- "I'm going to start making lunch."
- "The kitchen needs cleaning before dinner."
- "Looks like the toys are all over the floor."

**Then**: Let them join. Don't command.

### Use Responsibility as a Reward

> "Keep in mind that **working together with an adult is a privilege** for a child."

- Don't frame chores as punishment
- Frame contribution as membership on the team
- Helpful children get to be included in more

## Emotional Regulation Tools

### 1. **Practice Being Silent**

> "When in doubt, turn away."

- Silence is powerful
- Fewer words = more weight when you do speak
- Turning away removes the audience for misbehavior

### 2. **Keep Energy Low**

> "She's kept her energy low and the kids have followed her lead."

- Don't amp up to match their energy
- Stay steady, calm, grounded
- They will come down to meet you

### 3. **Model Tiredness for Bedtime**

The author's bedtime breakthrough:

> "Around eight o'clock each night, when things began to get wild, I decided to try the signals and turned down the lights. Model, very calmly: **'I'm tired. My body is telling me I'm tired. I'm going to bed.'**"

**The Process**:
1. **Model**: "I'm tired. I'm going to bed."
2. **Go to bed yourself**: Brush teeth, get in PJs, get in bed, read
3. **Acknowledge when they join**: Hug, smile
4. **Connect**: "Would you like to be a big girl now? Stay in bed."
5. **Help them settle**: Back rubs, calm presence
6. **Quiet cues**: "Let's be quiet and still so our bodies and minds can calm down and go to sleep."

**Result**: In 3 weeks, one of the most difficult parenting issues became "a nothing burger."

**Key**: Model the behavior. Don't command it.

## Acknowledge Helpfulness (Don't Praise)

> "Point out helpfulness and unhelpfulness. Instead of praising children for helping with a request, switch to acknowledging the overall helpfulness. **'That's helpful.'** A simple statement is all you need."

### Why This Matters

- Praise creates external validation dependency
- Acknowledgment reinforces contribution
- "That's helpful" > "Good job!" or "I'm so proud of you!"

### Practice Praise-Free Periods

> "Work your way up to two hours, eventually an entire day. This is praise-free moments."

**What You'll Notice**:
- Your words carry more weight
- Children listen better
- Less noise, clearer signal

## Yelling: It Teaches Anger, Not Behavior

> "American parents simply shoot themselves in the foot when they yell because in the end, **yelling doesn't teach children to behave. Instead, it teaches them to get angry**."

> "We train them to be angry and get upset and that yelling solves the problem. But by contrast, parents who **control their own anger**, both around and toward their children, help their children learn to do the same. They learn their emotional regulation from us."

### The Hard Truth

You're not just failing to teach the lesson when you yell. **You're teaching the opposite lesson**: that anger and yelling are how we solve problems.

### The Alternative

- Pause before responding
- Breathe
- Lower your voice instead of raising it
- Model calm even when frustrated

## When to Be Silent

These situations call for **minimal words, minimal expression**:

- **Tantrums**: Let them practice self-regulation
- **Whining**: Don't reward with attention
- **Testing boundaries**: Use "the look"
- **Minor misbehavior**: Silence or consequence puzzle
- **When you're frustrated**: Pause, breathe, then respond with few words

**Mantra**: "When in doubt, say less."

## The Comedido Toolkit

**How to Cultivate "Noticing What Needs to Be Done"**:

1. **Include them in real work** - Cooking, cleaning, yard work, repairs
2. **Let them observe first** - They don't have to "help" immediately; observation is learning
3. **Work alongside them** - Not "go do this"; do it together
4. **Hint, don't command** - "Dishes need washing" vs "Go wash the dishes"
5. **Acknowledge contribution** - "That's helpful" when they notice and act
6. **Make it real and doable** - Age-appropriate, actual contribution

**Over time**: They start to notice and do without being asked.

## Autonomy Zones: 3+ Hours Per Week

> "Kids should spend **at least three hours each week in autonomy zones**, and work your way up to a few hours each day, using time after school and on the weekends."

**What Is an Autonomy Zone?**
- Time without adult direction or supervision (within safe boundaries)
- Child decides what to do, how to do it, when to stop
- No scheduled activities, no hovering

**Why It Matters**:
- Builds self-governance
- Reduces anxiety
- Fosters creativity and problem-solving

**Examples**:
- Free play in backyard/room
- Walking to neighbor's house alone (if age-appropriate)
- Time at park with minimal parent intervention
- Self-directed projects or exploration

## Quick Reference: Tactics by Situation

### Child Won't Cooperate
- **Try**: Question instead of command ("What needs to happen?")
- **Try**: Just start the task yourself
- **Try**: Frame as communal activity ("Let's all...")
- **Don't**: Force or lecture

### Tantrum in Progress
- **Try**: Minimal words, minimal expression, maximum calm
- **Try**: Turn away, give space
- **Don't**: Lecture, negotiate, or match intensity

### Bedtime Battles
- **Try**: Model tiredness ("I'm tired. I'm going to bed.")
- **Try**: Go to bed yourself, let them follow
- **Don't**: Command, argue, or engage power struggle

### Demanding/Entitled Behavior
- **Try**: "What am I, your donkey?"
- **Try**: Silence - let them figure it out
- **Don't**: Jump to serve them

### Minor Misbehavior
- **Try**: "The Look"
- **Try**: Consequence puzzle, then walk away
- **Don't**: Over-explain or nag

## Remember

- **Fewer words = more impact**
- **Calm is contagious (so is chaos)**
- **Model the behavior you want to see**
- **Cooperation > obedience**
- **Tantrums = practice for self-regulation**
- **Helpfulness > praise**
- **Questions > commands**

**Your job**: Be calm. Use few words. Let them practice. Acknowledge contribution.
