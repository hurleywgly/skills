# Newborn to 6 Months: New Baby Preparation

**Context**: Preparing for a new baby. This is your prep guide and reference for the fourth trimester and early months.

---

## What You Already Know (From Your First Child)

- Sleep training works (12 Hours method)
- Consistent routine is key
- Patience pays off
- You can maintain your adult life while parenting

**Advantage**: You've done this before. Trust your experience.

---

## The Fourth Trimester (0-3 Months)

### What's Happening

**Baby's Perspective**:
- Massive transition from womb to world
- Still developing circadian rhythm
- Needs frequent feeding (every 2-4 hours)
- Sleep cycles are short and chaotic
- Seeking comfort and security

**Your Job**:
- Meet basic needs (food, sleep, comfort, safety)
- Begin establishing rhythms (not rigid schedules yet)
- Teach day/night distinction
- Build secure attachment

---

## Sleep: The Foundation

### Weeks 1-2: Observation

**From 12 Hours Method**:
- Log all feeds and sleep times
- Look for patterns
- No "training" yet - just data gathering

**Day/Night Distinction Starts NOW**:
- **Daytime**: Lights on, normal noise, social during feeds
- **Nighttime**: Dark, quiet, minimal interaction, all business

### Weeks 3-8: Building Rhythm

**Goals**:
- Longer stretches at night (3-5 hours)
- Begin bedtime routine
- Introduce The Pause (from Bringing Up Bebe)

**The Pause in Practice**:
- Baby fusses at night - wait 2-5 minutes before responding
- Listen: Escalating or settling?
- Many babies self-soothe within 5 minutes
- If escalating, respond with nighttime toolbox

### Weeks 8-12: The Push Toward 12 Hours

**Goals**:
- 12 hours straight sleep by 12 weeks (or close)
- 4 feeds during day, none at night
- Self-soothing skills developing

**How to Get There**:
- Strict bedtime routine (same every night)
- Put baby down AWAKE (drowsy but not asleep)
- Use nighttime toolbox consistently
- Gradual elimination of night feeds

---

## Feeding

### Breast or Bottle: Your Choice

**Either way, principles are the same**:
- Feed on demand in early weeks
- Gradually move toward schedule (by 8 weeks)
- Goal: 4 feeds per day by 12 weeks

### Schedule Progression

**Weeks 1-4**: On demand (usually every 2-3 hours)

**Weeks 5-8**: Begin stretching (every 3-4 hours during day)

**Weeks 9-12**:
- 4 feeds per day
- 4 hours apart
- 12-hour daytime window
- NO night feeds

**Example** (7am-7pm):
- 7am - Feed 1
- 11am - Feed 2
- 3pm - Feed 3
- 7pm - Feed 4 (bedtime feed)
- Sleep 7pm-7am

---

## The Bedtime Routine (Establish Early)

**Start by Week 2-3**:

1. **Same time every night** (choose your 12-hour block)
2. **30 minutes before final feed, go to nursery**
3. **Lower lights**
4. **Soft music**
5. **Bath** (warm, brief)
6. **Diaper + PJs**
7. **Read or sing** (short, calm)
8. **Final feed** (in nursery, dim light)
9. **Crib while AWAKE**

**Why This Matters**: Baby learns the sequence = sleep is coming. Security in predictability.

---

## The Nighttime Toolbox (Your Lifeline)

When baby wakes at night:

### 1. **The Pause** (2-5 minutes)
- Wait and listen
- Self-soothing?

### 2. **Gentle Touch** (if still fussing)
- Soft stomach/back strokes
- "Shh, shh" sounds
- DON'T pick up yet

### 3. **Pacifier** (if you use one)
- Offer, keep environment dark/quiet

### 4. **Diaper Check**
- Quick, efficient, no talking

### 5. **Feed** (if necessary in early weeks)
- Dark, quiet, businesslike
- Eliminate by 8-12 weeks

**Remember**: Each time you go straight to picking up/feeding, you delay self-soothing skills.

## Developmental Milestones (General)

### 0-3 Months

**Physical**:
- Lifts head during tummy time
- Tracks objects with eyes
- Brings hands to mouth
- Startles at loud sounds

**Social**:
- Begins to smile socially (6-8 weeks)
- Makes cooing sounds
- Recognizes familiar faces
- Calms when picked up

### 3-6 Months

**Physical**:
- Rolls over
- Reaches for toys
- Sits with support
- Brings objects to mouth

**Social**:
- Laughs
- Babbles
- Responds to name
- Shows excitement

**Remember** (from HGP + NurtureShock): Standardized milestones are guidelines, not gospel. Your baby's timeline is their own.

## What to Watch For (Medical)

**Normal Newborn Stuff**:
- Frequent feeding (8-12 times/day initially)
- Irregular sleep
- Fussiness in evenings
- Spitting up after feeds
- Newborn acne
- Umbilical cord stump healing

**Call Healthcare Provider If**:
- Fever >100.4 degrees F (rectal) in baby under 3 months
- Lethargy (hard to wake for feeds)
- Not wetting enough diapers (6+ wet/day by day 5)
- Jaundice (yellowing skin) that gets worse
- Persistent vomiting (not just spit-up)
- Inconsolable crying for hours

**Trust Your Gut**: You know when something feels off.

## Preparing Your Toddler for a New Sibling

**Your older child will be around 3 years old when baby arrives**

### Before Baby Arrives

1. **Talk about it simply**
   - "A baby is growing. Soon they'll be part of our family."
   - Read books about being a big sibling

2. **Don't overpromise**
   - Babies aren't instant playmates
   - "Baby will cry, eat, and sleep a lot at first"

3. **Involve them**
   - "Help" set up nursery
   - Pick out one thing for baby (stuffed animal, book)

### After Baby Arrives

**Your Toddler's World Just Shifted**:
- They're no longer the only child
- Parent attention is divided
- Routines may change

**How to Support Them**:

1. **Maintain THEIR routine** as much as possible
   - Same bedtime
   - Same meals
   - One-on-one time with each parent

2. **Acknowledge their feelings**
   - "It's different now with a baby here."
   - "Sometimes you wish it was just us again. I get it."

3. **Don't force "big sibling" role**
   - Let them be interested (or not)
   - No pressure to help or be gentle if they're not ready

4. **Watch for regression**
   - Sleep issues, potty training setbacks, clinginess
   - Normal and temporary
   - Don't shame, just support

5. **Give them jobs (comedido)**
   - "Can you bring me a diaper?"
   - Let them help (their way, not perfectly)

**From HGP**: Don't constantly intervene in sibling conflict (when age-appropriate). They're learning negotiation.

## Maintaining Your Adult Life (French/Danish Approach)

**The Trap**: Newborn = life stops, everything revolves around baby

**The Alternative**:
- Baby fits into YOUR rhythm (gradually)
- Preserve couple time (even 15 minutes after bedtime)
- Accept help (build alloparent network NOW)
- Don't lose your identity

**Practical**:
- Trade off baby duty so each parent gets breaks
- Ask family/friends for specific help (meals, holding baby while you shower, watching your toddler)
- Say yes when people offer
- Remember: Two parents alone is absurd. Use your village.

---

## Preparing for Regressions

**Reality**: Sleep will regress. Teething, growth spurts, illness, travel.

**When It Happens**:
1. **Back to basics** - Full routine, every step
2. **Use toolboxes** - Don't create new habits
3. **Check for medical issues** - Ear infection, teething, etc.
4. **Be patient but consistent** - Usually resolves in 3-7 days
5. **Support each other** - Hardest time to stay aligned, most important time
