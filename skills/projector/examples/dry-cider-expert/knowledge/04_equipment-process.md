## DIY Cost Reduction

If budget is tight:

- **Fermenter**: 1-gallon glass growlers ($3-8) or large Mason jars work for small batches
- **Airlock**: A balloon with pinhole works (less reliable but functional)
- **Bottles**: Save and reuse wine bottles, beer bottles, or swing-top bottles
- **Siphon**: Basic vinyl tubing ($3) works if you're careful about starting siphon

**Don't skimp on**: Sanitizer. Contamination ruins batches. Star San or One Step are worth the $10-15.

## Step-by-Step Fermentation Process

### Day 1: Setup

1. **Sanitize everything** that will touch juice or cider. Follow sanitizer instructions.

2. **Prepare juice**:
   - If using fresh-pressed: Add 50-100 ppm potassium metabisulfite (Campden). Wait 24 hours before pitching yeast.
   - If using store-bought: Check for sorbate (don't use if present). Can pitch yeast immediately if pasteurized.

3. **Record starting gravity** (if using hydrometer):
   - Pour sample into test jar
   - Float hydrometer, read at liquid surface
   - Record number (typical: 1.045-1.055 for apple juice)
   - This determines potential alcohol and confirms fermentation later

### Day 2: Pitch Yeast (or Day 1 if no sulfite)

1. **Hydrate yeast** (if using dry yeast):
   - Warm 1/4 cup water to 95-105°F
   - Sprinkle yeast on surface
   - Wait 15 minutes, stir gently
   - Add to juice

2. **Pour juice into sanitized fermenter** (leave 2-3 inches headspace)

3. **Attach airlock** filled with sanitizer or water

4. **Place in stable location** at 55-65°F

### Days 3-14: Primary Fermentation

- **Observe**: Bubbling should start within 24-48 hours
- **Don't disturb**: Resist opening or moving the fermenter
- **Monitor temperature**: Keep stable in 55-65°F range
- **Vigorous phase**: Days 3-7 typically show heavy bubbling
- **Slowdown**: Bubbling decreases after day 7-10

### Days 14-21: Confirm Completion

1. **Take gravity reading**:
   - Sanitize hydrometer and test jar
   - Draw sample with sanitized wine thief or siphon
   - Record reading

2. **Wait 3 days, take another reading**:
   - If identical, fermentation is complete
   - If still dropping, wait and retest

3. **Target final gravity**: Below 1.000 (often 0.995-0.998) for dry cider

### Week 3-4: First Racking

1. **Sanitize secondary vessel** (clean carboy or bucket)

2. **Siphon cider** from fermenter to secondary:
   - Place fermenter higher than secondary
   - Insert racking cane, avoiding sediment at bottom
   - Start siphon, fill secondary slowly to minimize splashing

3. **Leave sediment behind**: The yeast/apple debris (lees) stays in original vessel

4. **Top up if needed**: Minimize headspace. Some makers add similar cider or boiled/cooled water.

5. **Add sulfite**: 30-50 ppm potassium metabisulfite for oxidation protection

6. **Attach airlock**: Even though fermentation is complete, airlock prevents oxygen

### Months 2-6: Aging

- **Location**: Cool (50-60°F), dark, stable temperature
- **Check monthly**: Ensure airlock has liquid, no contamination signs
- **Rack again at month 3**: If significant sediment accumulates
- **Taste periodically**: Note how flavors develop and mellow

## Hydrometer Usage

The hydrometer is your most important measuring tool. It tells you:
- Starting sugar content (potential alcohol)
- Whether fermentation is complete
- Final alcohol content (calculated)

### How to Read

1. Fill test jar with cider sample
2. Gently lower hydrometer into liquid
3. Let it settle and stop spinning
4. Read number at liquid surface (not the meniscus curve)

### Key Numbers

| Reading | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| 1.045-1.055 | Typical starting gravity for apple juice |
| 1.000 | Water (no sugar) |
| 0.995-0.998 | Fully fermented dry cider |
| Above 1.000 at end | Residual sugar remains—not fully dry |

### Calculating ABV

**Formula**: (Starting Gravity - Final Gravity) × 131.25 = ABV%

**Example**:
- Starting: 1.050
- Final: 0.998
- (1.050 - 0.998) × 131.25 = 6.8% ABV

## Sanitation Protocol

Contamination is the #1 cause of failed batches. Follow this protocol:

### What to Sanitize

Everything that touches juice or cider after pressing:
- Fermenters, airlocks, stoppers
- Siphons, tubing, racking canes
- Hydrometers, test jars
- Bottles, caps, filling equipment
- Spoons, funnels, anything that goes in

### Sanitizer Options

| Product | Mix Ratio | Contact Time | Rinse? |
|---------|-----------|--------------|--------|
| **Star San** | 1 oz per 5 gallons water | 30 seconds | No rinse needed |
| **One Step** | 1 tbsp per gallon water | 2 minutes | No rinse needed |
| **Iodophor** | Follow package | 2 minutes | Optional rinse |

**Recommendation**: Star San. No-rinse, effective, and the foam is harmless.

### Protocol

1. Clean equipment first (remove visible debris)
2. Mix sanitizer in bucket or spray bottle
3. Submerge or spray all surfaces
4. Allow contact time
5. Don't rinse—residual sanitizer is safe and continues protecting

## Racking and Aging Timeline

### Suggested Schedule for Dry Cider

| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| Day 1 | Start primary fermentation |
| Weeks 2-3 | Confirm fermentation complete (stable gravity) |
| Week 3-4 | First rack to secondary |
| Month 3 | Second rack if needed (heavy sediment) |
| Month 6 | Taste evaluation, decide on bottling |
| Month 6-12 | Bottle or continue aging |

### Signs It's Time to Rack

- Heavy layer of sediment (lees) visible at bottom
- 3+ months since last rack
- Before bottling (always rack first)

### Signs of Problems

- Mold on surface (contamination—usually salvageable by removing carefully)
- Vinegar smell (acetobacter—likely lost)
- No airlock activity AND gravity still high (stuck fermentation)
