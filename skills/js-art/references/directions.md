# Direction library

A direction is four dials. Set them from a preset, from the user's description, or by mixing.

| Dial | What it decides | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Lineage** | Composition grammar and shape vocabulary | This file |
| **Medium** | Surface: how ink or paint behaves | `media.md` |
| **Palette** | 5–8 inks plus a paper color | Lineage default, the user's own, or color-first brief |
| **Brief** | What the picture is about, and how indirectly | `briefs.md` |

Hex values are starting approximations from known works, not sampled from scans. Items marked *(unverified)* came from memory during research. Sources are at the end.

## Presets

Ready-made dial settings. Offer three or four that fit the request, plus "describe your own" and "surprise me".

| Preset | Lineage | Medium | Brief style | Proven |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Print Shop** | Mid-century print (L0) | riso + gouache | Memory vignette or place-and-weather | Yes: used for the bundled example |
| **Dry Tools** | 1990s anime background (L9), "but original" | Model picks 3–5 dry media (pencil, pastel, conté) | Memory vignette, nothing literal | Yes: the original prompt this skill grew from |
| **Twilight Block** | Shin-hanga (L1) | woodblock | Place and weather | |
| **Park Poster** | WPA serigraph (L5) | screen | Place and weather | |
| **Scissors** | Matisse cut-outs (L2) | cutpaper | Music or color-first | |
| **Five Slabs** | Etel Adnan (L14) | palette-knife oil, or riso | Color-first | |
| **Big Bloom** | Scandinavian textile (L6) | screen on cloth | Object meditation | |
| **Blue Plate** | Cyanotype (L15) | cyanotype | Object meditation | |
| **Quiet Grid** | Agnes Martin (L12) over a pale field | graphite + thin wash | Constraint game | |
| **Surprise me** | Pick a lineage and a medium that the mixing rules say pair well, and that the user has not seen this session | | Ask one question for the brief | |

**Open-ended requests.** If the user describes a look in their own words ("like a 1970s ski poster", "my grandmother's kitchen curtains"), don't force a preset. Find the nearest one or two lineages, state the grammar you are borrowing from each in a sentence, and confirm. If nothing here fits, write a new lineage block in the same eight-part shape before drawing, and offer to save it to this file afterwards.

## L0. Mid-century print (the house direction)
Big flat shapes, wit, and visible ink.
- **Anchors:** Charley Harper, Mary Blair, Josef Albers, Alexander Girard.
- **Composition:** 3–9 very large shapes, at least one cropped by the frame. One compositional idea per picture (strata, nested fields, cropped circles, pattern over a landform, arcs from an off-canvas center). Asymmetric. One small warm accent against a cool field.
- **Shapes:** discs, wave bands, nested rectangles, boomerangs, kidney shapes, starbursts, harlequin diamonds, arcs.
- **Palette:** `#22305c` `#2f62b0` `#4aa3b8` `#9cc3d6` `#e3a535` `#e36a50` `#efbfae`; paper `#efe6d2`.
- **Tells:** misregistration, halftone fields, knocked-out white, dry-brush streaks, contour lines cut into flats.
- **Kills it:** literal objects (holiday props, records, houses); many small elements; warm inks printed over cool ones without a knockout (they turn brown).
- **Good for:** weather, seasons, light, calm wit. **Bad at:** realism, drama.

## L1. Shin-hanga woodblock landscape
Quiet weather and twilight, flat color inside a fine keyline.
- **Anchors:** Kawase Hasui (*Zōjō-ji in Snow*, 1925), Yoshida Hiroshi (*Sailing Boats* series), publisher Watanabe Shōzaburō.
- **Composition:** 3–4 stacked planes (foreground silhouette, water or ground, far hills, sky). Horizon at 1/3 or 2/3. One cropped foreground element breaks the frame edge. Depth from value steps and overlap, no linear perspective. One small warm accent.
- **Shapes:** pine clusters, roof wedges, sail triangles, banded reflections, snow caps, rain as parallel hairlines, poles.
- **Palette:** `#1F3A5F` `#5C7FA3` `#A9C0CF` `#D9703E` `#2B2B2B` `#7A8C6E`; paper `#EFE6D2`.
- **Tells:** bokashi (a short wiped gradient at the top of sky or water), baren streaks, speckle, faint woodgrain in flats, keyline of varying weight, slight color-to-line misfit.
- **Kills it:** smooth full-height gradients (bokashi is a short band); uniform-weight outlines; more than about 8 colors.
- **Good for:** snow, rain, dusk, moonlight, water. **Bad at:** pure abstraction, high energy.

## L2. Matisse cut-outs
Scissored color, leaf and coral forms dancing on blocks.
- **Anchors:** Henri Matisse (*The Snail*, 1953; *Blue Nude II*, 1952; *The Swimming Pool*, 1952; *Jazz*, 1947).
- **Composition:** ground is a patchwork of 4–8 slightly off-square rectangles. 8–20 organic shapes sit one per block or straddle seams. All-over, frieze-like, zero depth. Negative shapes count equally. Scale varies about 1:4.
- **Shapes:** algae fronds, lobed leaves, four-point stars, hearts, wavy bands, torn-edge squares.
- **Palette:** `#1E4FA3` `#E8452C` `#F2B705` `#2E8B57` `#F28C28` `#C2187A` `#111111`; paper `#F4EFE3`.
- **Tells:** streaky brushed gouache in the flats, pinholes, faceted scissor-cut edges, lifted corners, small patches on joins.
- **Kills it:** smooth bezier blobs (use polyline cuts with occasional corners); perfect symmetry; drop shadows.
- **Good for:** joy, summer, sea, garden. **Bad at:** atmosphere, night, subtlety.

## L3. Bauhaus lyric geometry
Musical arrangements of small geometric signs on a breathing ground.
- **Anchors:** Paul Klee (*Ancient Sound*, 1925; *Castle and Sun*, 1928), Wassily Kandinsky (*Composition VIII*, 1923; *Several Circles*, 1926), Anni Albers.
- **Composition:** two modes. Klee: an irregular hand-ruled grid of 8–14 columns, cell colors running from dark edge to glowing center. Kandinsky: 20–40 elements floating on a field, diagonal thrusts, one dominant off-center circle, clusters against empty areas.
- **Shapes:** haloed circles, triangles, checker patches, arcs, thin ruled lines, crescents, arrows.
- **Palette:** `#C8452F` `#E3A72F` `#2F4B7C` `#6B8E6B` `#8C5A7A` `#1C1C1C` `#D98E73`; ground `#E9DFC7` or dark `#2A2622`.
- **Tells:** pooled wash edges, wobbly ruled lines, ground showing through.
- **Kills it:** exact grid alignment; saturated primaries only; even spacing.
- **Good for:** play, music, night towns. **Bad at:** landscape realism.

## L4. Swiss Concrete art
Systematic color, rule-derived, calm and exact.
- **Anchors:** Max Bill, Richard Paul Lohse, Josef Müller-Brockmann (the arcs of the 1955 Beethoven poster).
- **Composition:** one stated rule (modular grid with color permutation, concentric arcs in a progression, rotation). Strict module, large margins, asymmetric balance, no depth.
- **Shapes:** squares, bars, quarter-arcs, stairs, diagonal half-squares, rings.
- **Palette:** `#D62B1F` `#F2C200` `#1D4E9E` `#1B8A5A` `#111111` `#E86A1C`; ground `#F3F1EA`.
- **Tells:** few. Slight ink mottle, paper tooth, fine edge bleed.
- **Kills it:** randomness without a system; heavy texture; gradients.
- **Good for:** modern rooms, rhythm. **Bad at:** warmth, story. Highest risk of looking digital, so use it as a hybrid base.

## L5. WPA / national-park serigraph
Monumental landscape in 5–8 flat inks.
- **Anchors:** the 14 National Park Service designs silkscreened in Berkeley, 1938–41, mostly anonymous; C. Don Powell is credited with several. Dorothy Waugh's 1930s NPS posters *(unverified)*.
- **Composition:** dark foreground trees or cliff frame a lit hero landform. Values posterized to 3–4 steps per hue. Strong diagonals, low viewpoint. Originals are portrait with a text band; for 16:9 drop the band and widen the vista.
- **Shapes:** conifer silhouettes, stepped mesas, geyser plumes, flat cloud slabs, shadow wedges, lake bands.
- **Palette:** `#1F3D3A` `#3E6E7A` `#E0A84B` `#C55A3A` `#7A4B36` `#F0E2B8` `#22313F`; paper `#EFE4C6`.
- **Tells:** opaque overprint, hand-cut stencil edges (slightly angular curves), screen-mesh tooth, registration gaps showing paper slivers.
- **Kills it:** blended shading; fake "vintage" distress; too many hues.
- **Good for:** mountains, canyons, forests, golden hour. **Bad at:** intimate or abstract subjects.

## L6. Scandinavian textile print
Huge confident botanicals in repeat.
- **Anchors:** Maija Isola (*Unikko*, 1964; *Kivet*, 1956) for Marimekko, Stig Lindberg, Josef Frank.
- **Composition:** motifs oversized (one flower is 30–50% of the height) and cropped by every edge. Half-drop repeat implied. 2–4 colors. No depth; overlaps make a third color.
- **Shapes:** poppy discs with dark centers, pebble ovals, leaf paddles, bent stems, broad stripes, dotted fillers.
- **Palette:** `#E4002B` `#F26A8D` `#111111` `#F2C230` `#2F6B4F` `#2B4C8C`; cloth `#F5F0E6`.
- **Tells:** one screen per color, weave tooth, darker overlap seams, brush-drawn wobble.
- **Kills it:** small motifs (reads as wallpaper); perfect ellipses; timid pastels.
- **Good for:** cheer, kitchen and living-room warmth. **Bad at:** landscape, quiet.

## L7. Orphism
Color as rhythm: concentric discs and arcs in simultaneous contrast.
- **Anchors:** Sonia Delaunay (*Prismes électriques*, 1914; *Bal Bullier*, 1913), Robert Delaunay (*Premier Disque*, 1913).
- **Composition:** 2–4 large overlapping disc systems, each split into quadrants with ring colors offset per quadrant. Arcs continue across neighbors. Complementary pairs sit side by side. All-over, shallow.
- **Shapes:** ring segments, half-discs, quarter-arcs, zigzags, stripe ladders, checker bits.
- **Palette:** `#D8352A` `#F08A1C` `#F3C614` `#1F5FA8` `#2E8C6A` `#7B3F8C` `#1A1A1A`; ground `#EDE3CF`.
- **Tells:** gouache scumble, directional brushing inside each segment, soft unruled edges, pencil construction arcs.
- **Kills it:** perfect vector rings; rainbow order; neon saturation.
- **Good for:** energy, music, celebration. **Bad at:** stillness.

## L8. Hilma af Klint
Monumental spiritual diagrams on a tinted field.
- **Anchors:** *The Ten Largest* (1907), *The Swan* series, *Altarpieces* (1915).
- **Composition:** one saturated flat field. 3–6 large biomorphic or geometric bodies loosely symmetric about a center, joined by loops. Or strict bilateral or quartered symmetry.
- **Shapes:** spirals, rosettes, concentric circles, split discs, snail shells, looping ribbons, pyramids, seed pods.
- **Palette:** `#E58A5A` `#F2B8A0` `#9BB7D4` `#F0D36B` `#7C6BA8` `#3B5B8C` `#F6F0E4`; fields `#E8946A`, `#B9A6CF` or `#1E1E1E`.
- **Tells:** matte tempera, patchy coverage, paper seams, hand-drawn circles, pencil underdrawing.
- **Kills it:** clip-art "sacred geometry"; glow effects; perfect symmetry.
- **Good for:** contemplation, large calm color. **Bad at:** scenes.

## L9. 1980s–90s anime background painting
Poster-color skies and sunlit structures with hard light.
- **Anchors:** Kazuo Oga, Shichirō Kobayashi and Kobayashi Production; Hiromasa Ogura *(unverified)*.
- **Composition:** real one- or two-point perspective, low horizon, 50–65% sky. Cumulus towers in 3 values. Crisp cast-shadow shapes. Foreground detail falls off to flat haze.
- **Shapes:** cumulus stacks, utility poles and wires, tiled roofs, foliage clumps (dark base plus two lighter dab layers), shadow parallelograms, guardrails.
- **Palette:** `#3C7FC0` `#8EC3E6` `#F4F1E6` `#4F8A4B` `#2C4A3A` `#D9B38C` `#6B6F8A`; board `#F2EEE2`.
- **Tells:** opaque poster color, dry-brush edges, flat-brush dabs, wet-blended sky, thin ruled highlight lines.
- **Kills it:** soft airbrush everywhere; lens flare or bloom; purple-pink "lofi" grading.
- **Good for:** summer afternoons, nostalgia, townscapes. **Bad at:** abstraction. The hardest lineage to draw in code: it needs layered brush-dab systems, not the print engine.

## L10. Katazome stencil dyeing
Bold stencil-cut motifs with white resist lines on dyed cloth.
- **Anchors:** Serizawa Keisuke, Samiro Yunoki *(unverified)*, Okinawan bingata.
- **Composition:** tiled panels (4, 6 or 12), a single centered emblem, or all-over repeat. Frontal, flat, heavy shapes filling their cell. Every shape is separated by a constant-width undyed line, because the stencil needs bridges.
- **Shapes:** rounded pictograms (pots, birds, trees, waves, mountains), scallops, dots, hatch fills.
- **Palette:** `#1F3A6B` `#B5382A` `#D9A12B` `#4E7A4A` `#6B4A2F` `#2A2A2A`; cloth `#EADFC8`.
- **Tells:** soft, slightly swollen white resist lines; dye brushed with gradation inside a shape; cloth slub; uneven uptake.
- **Kills it:** shapes touching (no resist gap); thin lines; generic "Japanese" clichés.
- **Good for:** seasonal emblem sets, calendars, still-life objects. **Bad at:** deep space.

## L11. Gee's Bend quilt improvisation
Syncopated strips and squares that refuse to square up.
- **Anchors:** Annie Mae Young, Loretta Pettway, Mary Lee Bendolph; "Housetop" and "Lazy Gal" patterns.
- **Composition:** pick one schema (concentric Housetop, vertical bars, or 2–4 unequal blocks), then break it. Strip widths vary, seams tilt 1–4°, one off-color piece interrupts, borders are unequal.
- **Shapes:** strips, squares, wedges, L-shapes, patched rectangles.
- **Palette:** `#2F3E5C` `#6F87A6` `#B23A2E` `#D8A93B` `#7A4A2E` `#E8E0CF` `#1E1E1E`; backing `#D9CDB4`.
- **Tells:** denim fade lines, corduroy ribs, puckered seams, hand-quilting stitch rows crossing pieces.
- **Kills it:** uniform strips; a clean block grid; craft-store brights.
- **Good for:** warm abstraction. **Bad at:** imagery.
- **Care:** this is a living community with copyrights and licensing. Borrow the improvisational grammar, never reproduce a specific quilt, and name it as inspiration.

## L12. Agnes Martin
Barely-there bands and pencil grids.
- **Anchors:** *Friendship* (1963), *Mountain I* (1966), the late horizontal-band paintings.
- **Composition:** full-bleed horizontal bands (5–20) or a fine grid that stops short of the edge. Near-symmetric. Value range under 15%.
- **Shapes:** bands, ruled lines, tiny rectangles, dots at intersections.
- **Palette:** `#E9E4DA` `#D9DEE3` `#F1D9CF` `#E6DDB8` `#C9CED1` `#9A9A96`; gesso `#F3F0E8`.
- **Tells:** graphite lines that skip over canvas weave; thin wash that stops short of the lines, leaving a halo.
- **Kills it:** crisp 1px lines; contrast; any motif.
- **Good for:** serenity, a TV that should disappear. **Bad at:** reading from across a room. On 16:9, center a square or call it "after Martin".

## L13. Eyvind Earle
Stylized hills and gothic trees with jewel detail on flat masses.
- **Anchors:** Earle's styling of *Sleeping Beauty* (1959) and his serigraphs from 1974 on.
- **Composition:** rolling horizontal hill bands against strict vertical trunks. Everything in focus. Dark foreground silhouette with a rim-lit edge. Near-black masses with dense micro-texture set against smooth gradient fields.
- **Shapes:** flat-topped oaks, columnar trunks, squared foliage slabs, contour-striped hills, fog bands, stippled grass.
- **Palette:** `#0E1B2A` `#1F4E5F` `#3F7F5A` `#C9A227` `#E36F2C` `#7B2D5B` `#F1E3B5`; ground `#10151C`.
- **Tells:** many-layer serigraph, razor edges, a thin luminous gradient within a band, repeated tiny highlight strokes.
- **Kills it:** soft edges; muted palette; skipping the micro-detail layer.
- **Good for:** dramatic dusk, forests, coast. **Bad at:** casual lightness.

## L14. Etel Adnan small landscapes
A mountain, a sun, five slabs of color.
- **Anchors:** her Mount Tamalpais paintings.
- **Composition:** 5–9 interlocking flat slabs. Horizon bands, one triangle or hump, often a square or disc sun. Shapes abut; no overlaps, no outline. Originals are small and near-square, so on 16:9 extend the bands instead of adding shapes.
- **Shapes:** humps, wedges, bands, squares, one disc.
- **Palette:** `#E9A23B` `#D9543A` `#7FA8B8` `#5B7F4F` `#F2D9C4` `#8C5E8A` `#2F4A6B`; canvas `#EFE7D6`.
- **Tells:** oil pulled with a palette knife in single swipes, knife ridges, slivers of raw canvas between slabs, thick edges.
- **Kills it:** too many shapes; gradients; tidy edges.
- **Good for:** warmth, optimism, reading instantly from a distance. **Bad at:** detail.

## L15. Cyanotype botanical
White specimen silhouettes on Prussian blue.
- **Anchors:** Anna Atkins, *Photographs of British Algae* (from 1843).
- **Composition:** one specimen centered with a generous margin, or 2–3 arranged. Life-size scale, no horizon. For 16:9, a triptych of plates on one sheet.
- **Shapes:** branching fronds (L-systems suit this), fern pinnae, seed heads, filament tangles, translucent overlaps.
- **Palette:** `#0F2F52` `#1C4E80` `#3F78A8` `#9DBFD6` `#E8F0F2`; sheet `#F1ECE0`.
- **Tells:** uneven brushed-emulsion borders, mottled blue, soft halos where the specimen lifted, semi-transparent thin parts, water stains.
- **Kills it:** crisp pure-white vectors; uniform blue; decorative plants that are not specimen-like.
- **Good for:** calm, scholarly, monochrome rooms. **Bad at:** color, drama.
- **Blueprint variant:** same blue with white ruled linework, dimension ticks and fold creases. Risks reading as a tech poster.

## Mixing rules

**Pair well**
- Shin-hanga composition with WPA serigraph inks: bokashi sky over stencil-cut land.
- Adnan slabs with riso or screen-print texture. Closest to the house direction.
- Matisse shapes on a katazome resist-line system. Both are cut-stencil logic.
- Earle silhouettes with shin-hanga bokashi and a twilight palette.
- Klee grid with Gee's Bend irregularity. Keep the textile tells or the watercolor tells, not both.
- af Klint diagrams with Delaunay ring segmentation.
- An Agnes Martin graphite grid as a top layer over any pale field.
- Cyanotype as a medium swap for Scandinavian or Matisse botanicals.

**Clash**
- Anime perspective with any flat lineage. The depth systems fight.
- Earle micro-detail with Adnan or Martin. Opposite densities.
- Swiss Concrete precision with heavy hand tells. Each reads as an error in the other.
- Orphism with Scandinavian florals. Two all-over rhythms compete.
- Two media tells from different processes in one piece (woodgrain plus canvas weave plus riso dots). Pick one substrate and one ink behavior.

**Always**
- 5–8 inks.
- Tie texture to the process: grain follows the block, brush follows the shape.
- Make imperfection systematic (one registration offset per layer), not per-pixel noise.

## Left out on purpose

- **Aboriginal dot painting.** The iconography belongs to specific Country, Dreaming stories and community rights. Work that looks Indigenous with no community connection is what the Fake Art Harms Culture campaign targets. Do not offer it; decline if asked and suggest another all-over pattern lineage.
- **Op art.** Fine line fields moiré and shimmer on TV panels.
- **Ellsworth Kelly.** Pure flat color on a screen reads as a default digital fill.
- **Memphis.** High kitsch risk on a wall.
- **Corita Kent, Saul Bass, Swiss posters proper.** They depend on typography.
